Word order ordstilling

The area examiners notice most. Get this right and your Norwegian sounds much more fluent.

A1Verb in second place inversjonIf anything other than the subject comes first, the subject moves after the verb.
Jeg jobber hjemme i dag.I’m working from home today.
I dag jobber jeg hjemme.Today I’m working from home.
I morgen skal vi til Bergen.Tomorrow we’re going to Bergen.
I dag jeg jobber hjemme.English word order – wrong in Norwegian.

Tip: Time words like i dag, nå, så, etterpå, derfor often start a sentence. Each one pushes the subject after the verb.

A1Questions spørsmålYes/no questions start with the verb. Other questions: question word, then verb.
Snakker du norsk?Do you speak Norwegian?
Har du vært i Tromsø?Have you been to Tromsø?
Hvor bor du?Where do you live?
Når begynner kurset?When does the course start?

Tip: There’s no “do” in Norwegian questions. Just move the verb to the front.

A1Where ikke goes in a main clauseIkke comes right after the first verb.
Jeg drikker ikke kaffe.I don’t drink coffee.
Jeg har ikke sett filmen.I haven’t seen the film.
Jeg kan ikke komme i dag.I can’t come today.
I dag kommer han ikke.He’s not coming today.

Watch out: a short pronoun object goes before ikke: Jeg kjenner ham ikke (I don’t know him). Other adverbs like alltid, aldri, ofte, også follow the same rule as ikke.

A2Where ikke goes in a subordinate clause leddsetningAfter at, fordi, hvis, når, som etc., ikke comes before the verb.
Jeg blir hjemme fordi jeg ikke har tid.I’m staying home because I don’t have time.
Han sier at han ikke kan komme.He says he can’t come.
Hun sier at hun alltid drikker te.She says she always drinks tea.
…fordi jeg har ikke tid.Main-clause order inside a subordinate clause.

Memory hook: in a subordinate clause, the order is subject – ikke – verb. Think “fordi jeg ikke…”, “at han ikke…”.

A2Subordinate clause firstThe whole clause counts as place 1, so the verb comes next, then the subject.
Når jeg kommer hjem, lager jeg middag.When I get home, I make dinner.
Hvis det regner, tar jeg bussen.If it rains, I take the bus.
Selv om jeg var trøtt, gikk jeg på kurset.Even though I was tired, I went to class.
Hvis det regner, jeg tar bussen.The verb must follow the comma.

Pattern: comma, then verb, then subject. “…, tar jeg”, “…, gikk vi”.

A1og, men, eller, så, for don’t change word orderThese five join two main clauses. Both halves keep normal order.
Jeg er trøtt, men jeg må jobbe.I’m tired, but I have to work.
Det regnet, så vi ble hjemme.It rained, so we stayed home.
Jeg er sliten, for jeg har ikke sovet.I’m tired, because I haven’t slept.

Trap: så meaning “then” is different and does cause inversion: Først spiser vi, så går vi en tur.

B1Indirect questions indirekte spørsmålA question inside a sentence becomes a subordinate clause: no inversion, ikke before the verb.
Hvor bor han? → Jeg vet ikke hvor han bor.I don’t know where he lives.
Kan du si meg når toget går?Can you tell me when the train leaves?
Hun spurte om jeg ville bli med.She asked if I wanted to come along. (yes/no question → om)
Jeg lurer på hvorfor han ikke kom.I wonder why he didn’t come.
Jeg vet ikke hvor bor han.Question word order inside a sentence.

Add som when the question word is the subject: Hvem kommer? → Jeg vet ikke hvem som kommer. Hva skjedde? → Fortell meg hva som skjedde.

B1Putting the object first emfaseStart with the object for emphasis or contrast. V2 still applies.
Den filmen har jeg sett før.That film I’ve seen before.
Kaffe drikker jeg ikke, men te liker jeg.Coffee I don’t drink, but tea I like.
Det visste jeg ikke!I didn’t know that!

Very common in speech: starting with det to refer back to what was just said: Det tror jeg ikke. Det er sant.

B2Small words, big meaning jo, vel, nok, daModal particles add attitude. They go before ikke.
Det er jo kaldt!It’s cold, as you know!
Du kommer vel?You’re coming, aren’t you?
Hun kommer nok ikke.She probably isn’t coming.
Kom da!Come on!
Det er jo ikke så farlig.It’s not that bad, after all.

In subordinate clauses they come before ikke and the verb: …fordi han nok ikke har tid.

B2Det-sentences presentering og utbrytningUse det to introduce something new, or to highlight one part of the sentence.
Det står en mann utenfor døra.There’s a man standing outside the door.
Det bor mange studenter her.Lots of students live here.
Det var Per som ringte.It was Per who called.
Det er i morgen (at) vi drar.It’s tomorrow that we leave.

New, indefinite subjects usually come after the verb with det: Det kom et brev i dag sounds more natural than Et brev kom i dag.

B2for å, uten å, ved åPreposition + å + infinitive – often where English uses -ing.
Jeg lærer norsk for å få nye venner.I’m learning Norwegian (in order) to make new friends.
Han gikk uten å si ha det.He left without saying goodbye.
Du lærer mye ved å lese aviser.You learn a lot by reading newspapers.
I stedet for å kjøre, tok vi toget.Instead of driving, we took the train.
Han gikk uten å sier ha det.Always the infinitive after å → uten å si.

Verbs verb

Good news: Norwegian verbs never change for person. Jeg, du, han and vi all use the same form.

A1Present tense presensAdd -r to the infinitive. Same form for everyone.
å snakke → jeg snakkerspeak → I speak

å gå → de gårwalk → they walk

Irregular: være → er, gjøre → gjør, vite → vet, si → sier, spørre → spør, and the modal verbs (kan, må, skal, vil, bør).

A2Past or perfect? preteritum / perfektumPast for a finished time. Perfect for no time given, or something still true now.
Jeg flyttet til Norge i 2019.I moved to Norway in 2019. (finished, time given)
Jeg har bodd her i sju år.I’ve lived here for seven years. (still true)
Har du vært i Bergen?Have you been to Bergen? (no time given)
I går har jeg sett en film.“I går” is a finished time → I går så jeg en film.

Shortcut: it works almost exactly like English “I saw” versus “I have seen”.

A1Future framtidskal for plans, kommer til å for predictions, or just present tense with a time word.
Jeg skal jobbe i morgen.I’m going to work tomorrow. (plan)
Det kommer til å regne i helga.It’s going to rain this weekend. (prediction)
Vi drar til London neste uke.We’re going to London next week.

Avoid: using vil for future. Jeg vil reise means “I want to travel”, not “I will travel”.

But: ordinary verbs do take å: Jeg liker å lese. Jeg pleier å ta toget. (I usually take the train.)

A1Imperative imperativDrop the final -e from the infinitive.
komme → Kom hit!Come here!
vente → Vent litt!Wait a moment!
gå → Gå rett fram.Go straight ahead. (no -e to drop)
Ikke glem nøkkelen!Don’t forget the key!
B1Past perfect pluskvamperfektumhadde + perfect form: something that happened before another past event.
Da jeg kom, hadde toget gått.When I arrived, the train had left.
Hun hadde aldri vært i Norge før hun flyttet hit.She had never been to Norway before she moved here.
Etter at vi hadde spist, gikk vi en tur.After we had eaten, we went for a walk.

Shortcut: works just like English “had gone”, “had been”.

B1Would, if, wish kondisjonalisville + infinitive for “would”. Past tense after hvis for unreal situations.
Hvis jeg hadde mer tid, ville jeg lese mer.If I had more time, I would read more.
Hvis jeg hadde visst det, ville jeg ha kommet.If I had known, I would have come.
Jeg skulle ønske jeg kunne bedre norsk.I wish I knew Norwegian better.
Jeg ville gjerne ha en kaffe.I’d like a coffee, please. (polite)

In speech, ha is often dropped: …ville jeg kommet. Both are correct.

B1Passive passivbli + perfect form for single events. -s on the verb for rules, habits and after modals.
Huset ble bygget i 1950.The house was built in 1950.
Huset ble tegnet av en kjent arkitekt.The house was designed by a famous architect. (by = av)
Butikken stenges klokka ni.The shop closes (is closed) at nine.
Skjemaet må fylles ut.The form must be filled in.

Signs and notices love the -s passive: Selges (for sale), Leies ut (for rent).

B1Particle verbs partikkelverbVerb + small word that changes the meaning, like English “give up”.

B2Det sies at… – formal passiveCommon in news and official texts. Recognising it matters most.
Det sies at …It is said that …
Det antas at …It is assumed that …

Saken skal behandles i morgen.The matter will be dealt with tomorrow.

Reading test: B2 texts use this a lot. If you see det + -s verb + at, think “it is … that”.
